# Adi Parva 04 — Pauloma Parva

Source: Mahabharata Volume 1.pdf. Verse numbers are this book's. Read from OCR. Quoted verses are checked against the page image.

## Chapter 4 — Entry into the story

PDF pp. 165-168. 12 verses. P. 167 also carries a picture captioned "Ugrashrava tells the Mahabharata story".

### Shravana

**Ugrashrava offers (vv. 1-2).** At Shaunaka's twelve-year sacrifice in Naimisharanya, Ugrashrava, son of Lomaharshana and a skilled Purana teller, comes to the sages. With folded hands he asks what they want to hear.

**The sages make him wait (vv. 3-8).** They say they will give him a fine topic and he will tell many stories, but Shaunaka, the head of the assembly, is still at the fire worship. He knows the divine stories of gods and asuras, and those of humans, Nagas and gandharvas (celestial musicians). He is able, steady in his vows, wise, and a teacher of the shastras and Aranyakas, truthful, self-controlled and given to tapas (austerity). All of them honour him. So wait until he has taken his seat, and then speak on whatever he asks.

**Ugrashrava agrees (v. 9).** "So be it." When the teacher is seated, he will tell whatever pure stories he is asked for.

**Shaunaka arrives (vv. 10-12).** Shaunaka finishes all his duties in order, pleasing the gods with Vedic praise and the ancestors with water. He comes to the sacrificial hall where the sages sit with the Suta's son placed in front. When the priests and members are seated, he sits too, and speaks. The chapter ends there, before his words.

**Differs from the popular version:**
- Nothing here contradicts a common assumption. The chapter has no episode beyond the seating.

**Unclear, flagged:**
- v. 3: the Sanskrit is compressed. I followed the Hindi.
- v. 6: the Hindi's brackets explain "shastra" as shruti, smriti, itihasa and Purana, and "Aranyaka" as the Brihadaranyaka and others. These are the translator's additions.

### Manana

#### Episode: Waiting for Shaunaka

- **L3 Psychological:** The sages are eager, and Ugrashrava is ready. Yet they hold back out of regard for one man, and he accepts it without any show of being slighted. Shared respect lets a whole hall wait comfortably.
- **L4 Ethical:** Even a good and wanted thing, a sacred story, has an order. Shaunaka's daily rite is not interrupted for it, and he finishes each duty before coming. Nothing is said to be wrong with starting early; the restraint is simply shown.
- **L5 Literary:** The chapter holds the story back. A teller is ready, an audience is ready, and then everyone sits, one by one, down to the last member. It ends on "and he said this", which pushes the reader into the next chapter.
- **L6 Historical & textual:** The text shows how it reaches a listener: a Suta tells it, in a hall of sages, on the question of the one presiding. The topic is set by the listener's asking, not by the teller's choice.

### Nididhyasana

Finish today's plain duty first, and sit down to the thing I'm eager for only when it is done.

## Chapter 5 — Puloma the danava comes to Bhrigu's ashram and talks with Agni

PDF pp. 169-174. 34 verses.

### In short

Shaunaka asks for the Bhrigu line first, and Ugrashrava traces it from Bhrigu down to Shaunaka's own forefather Shunaka. Asked how Chyavana got his name, he begins with Bhrigu's pregnant wife Puloma. While Bhrigu is away bathing, a demon also named Puloma arrives, claims she was promised to him, and presses the sacred fire to say whose wife she is. Agni, afraid of both lying and Bhrigu's curse, tells the truth, and the chapter ends there.

### Shravana

**Shaunaka chooses the topic (vv. 1-3).** Shaunaka asks whether Ugrashrava has studied all the Puranas, as his father did. The sages once heard these divine stories and first lineages from that father. Shaunaka wants the Bhrigu line first.

**Ugrashrava's source (vv. 4-8).** Ugrashrava says he knows the Purana as Vaishampayana and other great brahmins studied and told it, and he learned from his father what his father studied. He will describe the Bhrigu line, honoured by the gods, Indra, the rishis and the Maruts. As he has heard it, Brahma produced Bhrigu from fire at Varuna's sacrifice. Bhrigu's much-loved son was Chyavana.

**The line down to Shaunaka (vv. 9-11).** Chyavana's son was the righteous Pramati. Pramati and the apsaras (celestial nymph) Ghritachi had Ruru. Ruru and Pramadvara had Shunaka, Shaunaka's forefather, a master of the Vedas: austere, famous, truthful, self-controlled and measured in food and habits.

**Shaunaka's question (v. 12).** How did the great Bhargava come to be known as Chyavana?

**The demon arrives (vv. 13-21).** Bhrigu's beloved wife Puloma is pregnant. When Bhrigu goes out to bathe, a rakshasa (demon) also named Puloma comes to the ashram. Seeing her, he is overcome by desire and loses his senses. She takes him for a guest and invites him to a meal of forest fruits and roots. He is delighted, because he wants to carry her off, and says "my work is done." She had earlier been "chosen" (*vrita*) by him, but her father later gave her to Bhrigu by scriptural rite, and that wrong by her father rankles in the demon's heart always. Deciding this is his chance, he resolves to take her. Then he sees the fire burning in the fire-shrine.

**He questions the fire (vv. 22-29).** He asks Agni, on oath of truth, whose wife she is. Agni is the mouth of the gods, so he must answer rightly. He says he chose her first, and her father, acting falsely, gave her to Bhrigu afterwards. If she is Bhrigu's wife, Agni should say so, because he means to take her from the ashram. The anger burns his heart. He asks repeatedly, in doubt whether she is his or Bhrigu's. Agni dwells inside all beings and stands as a witness to merit and sin. Having heard the answer, he says, he will carry her off with Agni watching.

**Agni answers (vv. 30-34).** Agni is distressed, afraid of falsehood on one side and Bhrigu's curse on the other, and speaks softly. Yes, you chose her first, but you never married her by rite with mantras. Her father gave her to Bhrigu; he did not give her to you, out of greed for a better groom. Bhrigu then took her by the Vedic rite, with Agni himself as witness. "This is she, I know it. I cannot speak falsely."

> *bhīto 'nṛtāc ca śāpāc ca bhṛgor ity abravīc chanaiḥ* (v. 30)
>
> "Afraid of falsehood and of Bhrigu's curse, he spoke thus, softly."

> *nānṛtaṃ hi sadā loke pūjyate dānavottama* (v. 34)
>
> "For falsehood is never honoured in the world, best of danavas."

**Differs from the popular version:**
- A demon carrying off a sage's wife is easily assumed to be plain lust. Here he also holds a grievance, and he asks the sacred fire before acting.
- Shaunaka's question about Chyavana's name is not answered in this chapter.

**Unclear, flagged:**
- The demon is called *rakshasa* in the narration, and *danava* in the chapter heading and in Agni's address. The text does not explain the two labels.
- v. 18: the Sanskrit addresses the listener as both *brahman* and *rajan* (king). The Hindi keeps only *brahman*. The text does not explain *rajan*.
- v. 34: "this is she" does not spell out which claim Agni confirms. Vv. 31-33 point to Bhrigu's wife, but the line itself is compressed.

**Translator's note, kept separate from the text (p. 174, on v. 19):** When Puloma was a crying child, her father scolded her by saying, "O rakshasa, seize her." The demon, hiding in the house, silently took her as his wife. The note says that was all there was to it, and draws a lesson: do not say such things to children, even in jest or scolding, and do not name a rakshasa.

### Manana

#### Episode: The Bhrigu line

- **L6 Historical & textual:** The chain is stated: earlier brahmins such as Vaishampayana, then Ugrashrava's father, then Ugrashrava, with Bhrigu's birth marked "as we have heard" (v. 8). The listener is also the subject, since Ugrashrava is telling Shaunaka his own family's story.

#### Episode: The demon and the fire

- **L1 Theological:** Agni appears here as more than flame: mouth of the gods, dweller in all beings, witness of merit and sin. Yet this witness is distressed and afraid. The Divine here is bound by truth and does not simply overrule events.
- **L2 Adhyatmika:** The fire in the shrine is the same one said to live inside every being. Read inwardly, there is a witness within that cannot be made to say what I want, however hard I press it. The demon asks it again and again, already sure of his plan.
- **L3 Psychological:** The demon has an old wound and a fresh desire, and each feeds the other. He calls her father and Bhrigu liars while planning a theft, and he seeks a divine answer while announcing what he will do after it. He seems to want a verdict that makes his act feel like taking back what is his.
- **L4 Ethical (dharma-suksma):** Agni's dilemma is subtle. Silence or a lie would avoid helping the demon, but he will not lie, and the truth is asked for by someone who has said he will use it to abduct her. The text also grants part of the claim: he did choose first, and the father acted from greed. Yet it holds that the rite, not the prior wish, made the marriage. Puloma herself, who offered him hospitality, is never asked anything by anyone in this chapter.
- **L5 Literary:** The dominant feeling is *bhayanaka* (dread). A kind invitation of fruits and roots is answered by a private "my work is done", and the repeated questioning tightens the scene. A fire that speaks softly is an arresting image, and the chapter stops on Agni's answer, leaving her fate open.
- **L6 Historical & textual:** The Hindi adds emphasis to the Sanskrit in places. In v. 19 *abravit* (said) becomes "said in his mind". In v. 25 the Sanskrit "Bhrigu obtained" becomes "unjustly seized". V. 18 adds "alone". The footnote then narrows the "choosing" to a silent claim, which is weaker than the demon's own account.

### Nididhyasana

When I ask for an opinion today, first check whether I am seeking the truth or only permission for what I have already decided.
