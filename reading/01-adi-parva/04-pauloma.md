# Adi Parva 04 — Pauloma Parva

Source: Mahabharata Volume 1.pdf. Verse numbers are this book's. Read from OCR. No verse is quoted, so no page-image check was needed for quotation.

## Chapter 4 — Entry into the story

PDF pp. 165-168. 12 verses. P. 167 also carries a picture captioned "Ugrashrava tells the Mahabharata story".

### Shravana

**Ugrashrava offers (vv. 1-2).** At Shaunaka's twelve-year sacrifice in Naimisharanya, Ugrashrava, son of Lomaharshana and a skilled Purana teller, comes to the sages. With folded hands he asks what they want to hear.

**The sages make him wait (vv. 3-8).** They say they will give him a fine topic and he will tell many stories, but Shaunaka, the head of the assembly, is still at the fire worship. He knows the divine stories of gods and asuras, and those of humans, Nagas and gandharvas (celestial musicians). He is able, steady in his vows, wise, and a teacher of the shastras and Aranyakas, truthful, self-controlled and given to tapas (austerity). All of them honour him. So wait until he has taken his seat, and then speak on whatever he asks.

**Ugrashrava agrees (v. 9).** "So be it." When the teacher is seated, he will tell whatever pure stories he is asked for.

**Shaunaka arrives (vv. 10-12).** Shaunaka finishes all his duties in order, pleasing the gods with Vedic praise and the ancestors with water. He comes to the sacrificial hall where the sages sit with the Suta's son placed in front. When the priests and members are seated, he sits too, and speaks. The chapter ends there, before his words.

**Differs from the popular version:**
- Nothing here contradicts a common assumption. The chapter has no episode beyond the seating.

**Unclear, flagged:**
- v. 3: the Sanskrit is compressed. I followed the Hindi.
- v. 6: the Hindi's brackets explain "shastra" as shruti, smriti, itihasa and Purana, and "Aranyaka" as the Brihadaranyaka and others. These are the translator's additions.

### Manana

#### Episode: Waiting for Shaunaka

- **L3 Psychological:** The sages are eager, and Ugrashrava is ready. Yet they hold back out of regard for one man, and he accepts it without any show of being slighted. Shared respect lets a whole hall wait comfortably.
- **L4 Ethical:** Even a good and wanted thing, a sacred story, has an order. Shaunaka's daily rite is not interrupted for it, and he finishes each duty before coming. Nothing is said to be wrong with starting early; the restraint is simply shown.
- **L5 Literary:** The chapter holds the story back. A teller is ready, an audience is ready, and then everyone sits, one by one, down to the last member. It ends on "and he said this", which pushes the reader into the next chapter.
- **L6 Historical & textual:** The text shows how it reaches a listener: a Suta tells it, in a hall of sages, on the question of the one presiding. The topic is set by the listener's asking, not by the teller's choice.

### Nididhyasana

Finish today's plain duty first, and sit down to the thing I'm eager for only when it is done.
