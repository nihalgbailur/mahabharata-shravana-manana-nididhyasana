# Adi Parva 02 — Parvasangraha Parva (the Summary of the Parvas)

Source: Mahabharata Volume 1.pdf. Verse numbers are this book's. Read from OCR, with quoted verses checked against the page images.

## Chapter 2 — Samantapanchaka, the size of an akshauhini, summary of the parvas, and the fruit of hearing and reading the Mahabharata

PDF pp. 84-134. 396 verses.

### Shravana

**Samantapanchaka (vv. 1-16).** The sages ask Ugrashrava about the place he mentioned. At the junction of the Treta and Dvapara ages, Parashurama, driven by rage, repeatedly killed the Kshatriyas (warrior class) and made five lakes of their blood at Samantapanchaka. He offered that blood to his ancestors. The ancestors, led by Richika, were pleased with his devotion and valour and offered a boon. He asked to be freed from the sin of what he did in anger, and for the lakes to become famous tirthas (sacred places). They agreed and told him to spare the rest, and he stopped. At the junction of Kali and Dvapara, the Kuru and Pandava armies met in that same place, called free of the land's faults and highly righteous. Eighteen akshauhinis gathered there and perished there.

**The akshauhini (vv. 17-29).** A patti is 1 chariot, 1 elephant, 5 foot soldiers and 3 horses. Tripling at each step gives senamukha, gulma, gana, vahini, pritana, chamu and anikini; ten anikinis make an akshauhini: 21,870 chariots, 21,870 elephants, 109,350 foot soldiers and 65,610 horses. Eighteen of these died there, with the Kauravas as the occasion and wondrous Time as the mover.

**The war in days (vv. 30-32).** Bhishma fought ten days, Drona five, Karna two, Shalya half a day, then half a day of mace combat. At night Ashvatthama, Kritavarma and Kripa killed Yudhishthira's sleeping army.

**Praise and the first list (vv. 33-84).** As the self is among things to know and life among things dear, so is this history among all scriptures. No story on earth exists without it, as no body survives without food. Poets serve it as ambitious servants serve a noble master. Then comes the list of 100 parvas, from Paushya through the Bhagavad Gita, the Sanatsujata and the Anugita to Svargarohana, then Harivamsha as a khila (supplement) with Vishnu Parva and Bhavishya. Vyasa composed the hundred, and Ugrashrava told them at Naimisharanya as eighteen.

The war lasted eighteen days (v. 381).

**The closing praise (vv. 382-396).** One who knows the four Vedas with limbs and Upanishads but not this is not truly discerning. It is artha-shastra, dharma-shastra and kama-shastra (the Hindi adds moksha-shastra in a bracket). After it, other hearing tastes like a crow after a cuckoo. The Puranas stand within it. Hearing it equals giving a hundred gold-horned cows daily. A Brahmin's day-sins and night-sins are shed by reciting it at the junctions of day. Hearing this summary first makes entering the epic as easy as crossing the salt sea by boat.

**Differs from the popular version:**
- Krishna at Bhishma: the text says whip in hand, not the chariot wheel people often assume.
- Krishna's offer: the chosen Krishna is a non-fighting counsellor (saciva, mantri). The word charioteer isn't used here.
- Bhima, not Draupadi, is the one named as mocking Duryodhana, and that mockery is given as the cause of the dice.
- The summary credits Dhritarashtra with lifting Draupadi out of the dice "sea". No rescue by Krishna is mentioned in this chapter.
- People assume only the Kauravas' plot burned in the lac house; here Purochana and a sleeping Nishada woman with five sons also burn.
- Karna's birth secret comes out at the water rites after the war, in this summary.

**Unclear, flagged:**
- v. 369: the Sanskrit gives 320 verses for Mahaprasthanika (checked on p. 130). The Hindi gives 123. Used the Hindi in the table and noted the conflict.
- The eighteen parvas total 1,933 chapters and 84,639 verses; with the Harivamsha, 96,639. This chapter gives no grand total. If 320 is right, 96,836.
- vv. 166 and 201 both tell of Indra taking Karna's earrings, at two different places in Aranya. Unexplained.
- v. 15 names the place from the armies meeting their end; note 2 (p. 134) derives "samanta" from "those gathered, their end". v. 11 ties it to the five lakes. Both stand on these pages.
- v. 132: "kinkaras" are called rakshasas by the Hindi. Nothing further here.
- v. 172: Somaka sacrifices one son for a hundred. Listed without explanation.
- v. 366: Lauhitya is rendered "red sea" by the Hindi. Location not given.
- v. 236: Karna refuses "shauṭiryat", which the Hindi gives as ego. The Sanskrit word may not be that negative; I can't settle it from these pages.
- v. 84: the Hindi says Ugrashrava arranged the hundred into eighteen. The verse only says he told eighteen.
- The 100 parvas in vv. 42-83: I did not count them to confirm the total of 100.

### Manana

#### Episode: Parashurama's blood lakes and the battlefield

- **L2 Adhyatmika:** The same ground holds the worst anger and then becomes sacred. He asks to be freed and the place becomes tirtha. What I did in anger may not be erasable, but it can be turned to something by owning it as sin.
- **L3 Psychological:** He has already done it all before he says the word "sin". Rage is called a swoon (krodha-murcchita), so he isn't choosing clearly. Only the ancestors' approval gives him the room to ask for freedom. Then they have to tell him to stop.
- **L4 Ethical:** Dharma-sukshma: the ancestors praise his devotion and valour and in the same breath tell him to forgive the rest. Devotion to one's dead does not make the massacre right, and he himself calls it sin. Praise of motive is not a licence for the act.
- **L5 Literary:** Bibhatsa (disgust) and raudra (fury) open a chapter that is mostly lists. The war's ground is introduced by an older slaughter, so that before any name of the war appears, the place has seen this already.

#### Episode: The numbers of the akshauhini

- **L3 Psychological:** The sages ask for the arithmetic, and Ugrashrava gives it with pride in exact counts. Exactness steadies the mind against something too vast to feel.
- **L5 Literary:** Karuna (grief) by arithmetic. Each akshauhini is counted cleanly to the last horse, and then v. 29 says all of them came there and ended. The dryness of the numbers is what makes the verse hit.
- **L1 Theological:** v. 29 names Time as the doer and the Kauravas as the occasion. Sauptika then says the survivors lived by resting on Krishna's strength (v. 301). The chapter puts these side by side without saying how they relate.

#### Episode: The choice at Upaplavya (vv. 218-221)

- **L1 Theological:** Krishna offers himself unarmed on one side and an army on the other, and lets each choose. He doesn't push. He is available as counsel only, and the chooser reveals himself.
- **L2 Adhyatmika:** Force I can count against wisdom that won't lift a weapon. I make this choice whenever I pick more resources over better guidance.
- **L3 Psychological:** The Hindi calls Duryodhana ignorant of his own interest. He takes what looks like strength. Arjuna asks for Krishna even on the stated condition that he won't fight. The text doesn't give more motive than this.
- **L4 Ethical:** The very next verses show Duryodhana taking Shalya by gifts on the road (vv. 222-223), and Shalya keeps the word he gave. A promise obtained by deceit still binds him. That is a subtle spot I'd like to see the full chapter handle.

#### Episode: Krishna down from the chariot (vv. 246-250)

- **L1 Theological:** The one who removed Arjuna's delusion with reasons pointing to liberation then leaps at Bhishma, whip in hand, without fear, when he sees Arjuna slack. Teaching and direct action come from one person within a few lines, and the text calls him devoted to Yudhishthira's welfare.
- **L3 Psychological:** A lash of words to Arjuna follows ("vakya-pratoda", the goad of speech). Then Arjuna acts. The summary suggests that shame moved what teaching alone did not.
- **L4 Ethical:** The Hindi of v. 250 has Shikhandi placed in front so Arjuna can bring down Bhishma. The text states it without comment. Note it, and wait for the full chapter.

#### Episode: The end of the Vrishnis and Arjuna's failed Gandiva (vv. 354-362)

- **L1 Theological:** Rama and Keshava, though able (says the Hindi), do not override Time when it comes. The Hindi's bracket adds that this was to keep the sages' word true. Even the Lord here lets the course run.
- **L2 Adhyatmika:** "Anityata of powers" is the phrase: his weapons leave him. What I'm good at will also not stay. Arjuna's turn to renunciation comes from seeing that.
- **L5 Literary:** Bibhatsa and karuna. The greatest archer can't protect the women on the road, in two lines, without commentary.

#### Episode: Yudhishthira, the dog and hell (vv. 366-375)

- **L2 Adhyatmika:** He walks on without looking back at those who fell, and then refuses heaven without a dog. I can't yet tell from a summary how these fit together, but both are tests of what he's attached to.
- **L4 Ethical:** "Anrishamsya" (non-cruelty) is named as the reason he refuses to board. Dharma appears only after he refuses. Non-cruelty to the least creature outranks reward here.
- **L5 Literary:** Adbhuta (wonder). The hell scene is said to be shown "by a device" (vyajena), a last twist. The reader has been told this is coming, so what's left to find out is why.

#### Episode: How the text says it reached me

- **L6 Historical & textual:** Vyasa composed 100 parvas, and Ugrashrava told them as eighteen at Naimisharanya. The chapter counts every parva's chapters and verses, a text auditing itself. It also records that Harivamsha is a supplement (khila). Two passages show Sanskrit and Hindi disagreeing, v. 369 on the count and v. 84 on "arranged". The Hindi also adds moksha-shastra to v. 383 in brackets.

Verses:

v. 382, p. 131: *yo vidyāc caturo vedān sāṅgopaniṣado dvijaḥ / na cākhyānam idaṃ vidyān naiva sa syād vicakṣaṇaḥ* — "The twice-born who knows the four Vedas with their limbs and Upanishads, yet does not know this narrative, is not truly discerning."

vv. 391, pp. 132-133: *dharme matir bhavatu vaḥ satatotthitānāṃ / sa hy eka eva paralokagatasya bandhuḥ / arthāḥ striyaś ca nipuṇair api sevyamānā / naivāptabhāvam upayānti na ca sthiratvam* — "May your mind rest on dharma as you keep rising; it alone is the kin of one gone to the next world. Wealth and women, even when served by the skilful, do not become trustworthy nor lasting."

v. 396, p. 133: *ākhyānaṃ tad idam anuttamaṃ mahārthaṃ / vijñeyaṃ mahad iha parvasaṃgraheṇa / śrutvādau bhavati nṛṇāṃ sukhāvagāhaṃ / vistīrṇaṃ lavaṇajalaṃ yathā plavena* — "This great, unsurpassed, deeply meaningful narrative is to be understood here through the summary of parvas; heard first, it becomes easy for people to enter, as the wide salt water is by boat."

### Nididhyasana

Where I'm choosing today between more force and better counsel, take the counsel, even if it comes with no promise to fight for me.
